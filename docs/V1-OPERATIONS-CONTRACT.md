# V1 operational reporting and alert contract

Version: 1.0.0-policy.1, accepted 2026-10-04. Owner: Infrastructure, coordinated with Backend. Tracks [SECUR4ALL-178](https://andmorethings.youtrack.cloud/issue/SECUR4ALL-178); implementation remains [SECUR4ALL-237](https://andmorethings.youtrack.cloud/issue/SECUR4ALL-237) and [SECUR4ALL-243](https://andmorethings.youtrack.cloud/issue/SECUR4ALL-243).

This is the owner-approved V1-0 policy, not an assertion that the reporting service or wider V1 access authority is deployed. The owner approved Chicago 08:00 daily reporting, 7/14/90-day retention, service alert thresholds, correction cadence and notification limits on 2026-10-04; existing product/accounting decisions are carried forward separately. They do not grant cloud access, set customer allowances or decide customer charging.

## Established requirements and current evidence

- Every consumer feature requires an account. Built-in checks remain available when cloud access ends. External work requires authoritative trial, paid or complimentary access; research consent never buys access.
- A logical user check can involve multiple internal attempts/providers but must not cause duplicate deductions on retries. Operational counts, provider costs and customer charging are separate measures.
- Alerts go to **support@andmorethings.com**. The Dev resolver SNS subscription and a direct SNS test were confirmed. This proves that email path, not every alarm transition or a human response SLA.
- Earlier private Dev resolver qualification recorded Python 3.14, alarms for at least one runtime error or throttle in five minutes and at least five partial resolutions in five minutes, missing data non-breaching, and alarm/recovery transitions configured for the confirmed topic. This policy review does not requalify the current live alarm state or actual transition delivery. Those resolver invocation metrics are not user-check counts.
- No submitted message, URL, QR image/payload, account/device identifier, research demographic, token, provider response or model explanation belongs in operational reports or logs.
- User recovery guidance is automated. Operational escalation below means service remediation; it does not offer a human scam-analysis service.

## Sources and ownership

Backend emits versioned, validated check/attempt lifecycle facts only after authoritative access and idempotency decisions. Pre-admission `request_rejected` is a separate immediate fixed-code aggregate from trusted ingress; it has no per-request reference, user-derived fields, access class or check reconciliation row. Android/iOS must not directly increment billable or outcome counters. Lambda owns reconciliation and aggregation logic. Infrastructure owns bounded storage, scheduling, IAM, alarms, delivery configuration, redaction verification and the runbook; any report/delivery Lambda code stays with the Lambda agent. The product owner approves reporting policy; the support mailbox receives notifications.

`SECUR4ALL-190/110/120/121` own result semantics; `230/231/232/241` own access/accounting. Governed History adds a minimized result receipt; reopening a result is not a new check. This reporting work must not enable or alter History, analysis, deletion, export, billing, device binding or customer access gates. The report consumer must reject unknown schema versions, extra fields and invalid enum transitions without retaining raw rejected payloads in any log, table, queue, quarantine or DLQ. Retain only fixed allowlisted rejection counters; any valid envelope used for reconciliation keeps its original acceptance deadline. Validation failures produce a fixed counter. No permissive fallback to legacy FREE balances.

## Minimal event projection (proposed)

The versioned event vocabulary is `check_accepted`, `check_terminal`, `request_rejected`, `attempt_started` and `attempt_finished`. It is a backend-to-operations contract, not a mobile HTTP endpoint. Reconciliation/backfill replays the same authoritative fact identity/revision with trusted producer-mode metadata, not a new counted `check_reconciled` event. `delivery_status` is infrastructure-internal notification state outside this Lambda lifecycle schema. Lambda owns its executable schema and publisher; the following is the required privacy boundary, not a second wire schema.

The detailed candidate schema is owned by Lambda. For rejection aggregates, allow only trusted fixed environment/service/reason, bounded server-time window and count; never retain raw rejected input or per-request identifiers. For admitted check/attempt facts, allowlist fields: schema version; server event time; environment; service; operation kind (`message`, `url`, `qr_url`, `recovery_clarification`); access class (`trial`, `paid`, `complimentary`); lifecycle/outcome/reason enums; finite provider name; attempt index; duration bucket; integer provider request units; nullable estimated cost in integer micro-USD; terminal revision. Reject arbitrary tags, free text and negative/unbounded counters. Do not make request identifiers CloudWatch metric dimensions.

A restricted reconciliation store may additionally hold random server-generated event/check/attempt references and acceptance time. They must not encode subject information, URLs, user-supplied IDs or hashes of content. References exist solely for deduplication and corrections, are excluded from reports/metrics/email, and remain potentially linkable operational data while source mappings exist. They are not anonymous research records. Apply access controls and deletion semantics agreed in the account-data contract; do not duplicate subject mappings in this store.

Owner-approved retention (2026-10-04): linkable event/reconciliation records **at most 7 days from authoritative acceptance**; fixed-code diagnostic logs 14 days (matching current consumer/resolver configuration); identifier-free daily aggregate revisions 90 days. The old draft’s 30-day linkable retention is withdrawn. New store backups, queues, rejected-event handling and restore paths must not extend the seven-day linkable window; initially require no PITR/snapshots/DLQ copies for the new reconciliation store. Existing authority backups retain their separately approved lifecycle and are not duplicated here. Maintain account-deletion lookup through the existing authority boundary, with no second identity map in the operations store. Account deletion must erase still-linkable operations records before removing that lookup. Concurrent ingestion, delayed events, retry/redrive, backfill and restored data must be fenced by the existing authoritative deletion/receipt state; no ingestion may recreate erased or expired records. SEC237/243 must prove atomic admission against that fence and restored-backup suppression, including missing-authority cases, before activation. This policy does not approve a new identity map or additional tombstone retention; if existing lifecycle controls cannot satisfy the fence, bring the missing retention/design decision back for review. Aggregate records must contain no linkage or content and are not a claim of anonymous research data. TTL is a cleanup mechanism, not proof of exact deletion: expire access at the retention boundary, verify physical deletion and document backup lifecycle before activation. No new tables or retention changes are deployed by this document.

## Counting and reconciliation

- `received`: unique logical checks whose authority admission successfully created an `ADMITTED` check receipt in the reporting cohort. HTTP receipt, prepare/reserve attempts and pre-admission rejections do not establish acceptance. Replayed idempotent submissions do not increment it. Raw HTTP requests, validation rejections and technical attempts have separate counters. An embedded URL or QR redirect/provider fan-out attaches to the original logical check; internal child services do not create new accepted user checks.
- `completed`: authoritative terminal processing outcome `complete`. `inconclusive` is a separate terminal outcome with zero deduction; uncertain verdicts are not automatically service failures. Accountable complete processing still requires authoritative settlement evidence before any deduction is reported.
- `partial`: terminal assessments with explicitly incomplete coverage. They cannot be relabeled completed merely because a provider returned some data.
- `failed`: authoritative terminal outcome `failed`. Post-admission `blocked`, `invalid_input`, `unsupported` and `unavailable` are separate terminal categories, each with zero deduction after confirmed settlement. Operational technical-failure reasons are a separate allowlisted projection; do not label hostile input, policy blocking or unsupported features as an outage. There is no emitted V1 cancellation outcome. Closing the app cannot create a cancelled check.
- `pending`: accepted checks with no authoritative terminal record at the report's `asOf` time. Pending is not silently converted into failed when a client timeout occurs.
- For each cohort: `received = completed + partial + inconclusive + failed + blocked + invalid_input + unsupported + unavailable + pending`. Access-denied, invalid and rate-limited requests rejected before acceptance are outside this denominator and reported separately.
- Report each terminal outcome count and its fraction of `received` with explicit labels, numerator and denominator. Separately report `technical_failure_checks / received` based on approved fixed reason categories; this is different from the service alert’s recent terminal-check denominator. With zero received checks, show `N/A`, not 0% or 100%. Never describe inconclusive verdicts as confirmed threats or all incomplete checks as provider failures.
- Deduplicate identical events and attempt IDs. Out-of-order events are reconciled by valid monotonic lifecycle revisions; arrival time cannot overwrite a newer terminal outcome. Contradictory terminal revisions raise a reconciliation fault. Never multiply logical counts to compensate for lost events.
- Provider attempts and cost belong to their authoritative dispatch/start event-time period; later finish/cost facts revise that period rather than moving the attempt into the finish day. Label them separately from the accepted-check cohort. Sum only unique server attempt references; a conflicting duplicate of the same attempt fact phase/revision (provider, units, outcome, event time or cost) is a reconciliation fault, not another attempt or last-write-wins update. Start-to-finish enrichment is a valid lifecycle transition, while provider identity and authoritative start time remain immutable. Classify each observed attempt once as succeeded, failed, denied before dispatch, or unresolved/unknown, with `unique_attempts = succeeded_attempts + failed_attempts + denied_attempts + unresolved_attempts`. An execution attempt denied before dispatch is not an actual provider request; its zero units/cost require positive denial evidence. Keep dispatch-not-proven separate from proven dispatch; require a durable authoritative `attempt_started` reservation/fact **before** provider dispatch, later reconciled by `attempt_finished` using the same attempt reference. A crash after start remains one unresolved attempt with unknown units/cost until reliable reconciliation; absence of a finish event cannot erase the attempt or prove no cost. A missing cost or request-unit count is `unknown`, never zero. For provider-call attempts only, publish known estimated cost, known-cost attempt count, unknown-cost attempt count, known request-unit sum and unknown-unit attempt count; internal component attempts are separate and excluded from provider-cost coverage; do not present the known subtotal as total spend. Label estimates and coverage; invoices remain authoritative.
- Customer deductions/refunds come from the accounting ledger. Reports observe ledger outcomes but cannot decide charging, issue credits or restore allowances. **Processing completion is separate from chargeable completion.** Partial, failed and inconclusive assessments deduct zero; completed usable assessments follow their approved settlement contract. A settled recovery clarification still counts when the client loses its response. A retry or History reopen never produces a new deduction.
- Report accounting as `deducted_checks`, `no_deduction_checks`, and `settlement_unknown_checks` within accepted cohorts. `check_terminal` is published only from a confirmed authoritative settled receipt; unfinished or uncertain settlement remains pending/unknown even if an evaluator response exists. In this summary pending checks belong to settlement-unknown coverage, not a known zero deduction. Complimentary bypass checks have no allowance deduction and are separately counted as completed service use, without identities. Do not report an unresolved reservation as a completed deduction. Any mismatch between projected outcomes and the authoritative ledger raises a reconciliation fault instead of silently changing either.
- Local-only intake failures are not accepted server checks. V1 has no approved client analytics upload for them: show this coverage as **not collected**, never as zero failures. If a future client diagnostic path is proposed, its separate consent/purpose, schema, retention and authorization must be reviewed first.
- Provider cost is separately tracked even for nonchargeable or retried work. A rules-only check has zero external provider request units only when the producer proves no dispatch; lack of cost telemetry is `unknown`. AWS/store fees and subscription revenue are separate aggregate sources with explicit coverage, not estimates inferred from customer deductions.

## Daily report semantics

Owner-approved schedule (2026-10-04): reporting timezone `America/Chicago`; one daily report at 08:00 local time for the prior local calendar day. Compute half-open local-midnight boundaries using the IANA timezone, then store their UTC instants. Daylight-saving days have 23 or 25 hours and must not be treated as fixed 24-hour periods.

Each report includes environment, local date/timezone, exact UTC range, report revision, generated/as-of times, received and all lifecycle counts, clear failure reasons, rejection/attempt totals, provider-unit estimates and unknown-cost coverage, and a data-completeness indicator. Do not put check-level samples in email. Report Dev and Prod separately; no cross-environment totals.

Pending checks remain attributed to their acceptance day. Late terminal outcomes revise that original cohort before the seven-day reconciliation deadline for each check. Publish a new immutable report revision referencing the previous revision and delta; never silently edit a delivered report or count the check in today's received total. Coalesce corrections into the next scheduled daily email, with at most one correction revision per still-eligible cohort per run, so late events do not each send a message. Label late accounting/cost adjustments separately. Completed retention/deletion must not reverse an already counted outcome; if correction linkage has expired or been erased, disclose that corrections are no longer possible rather than reconstructing identifiers. After expiry, record an uncorrelated late-event counter and disclose the correction limitation. A failed report delivery retries twice with backoff and an idempotent `(environment, date, revision)` delivery key. Exhaustion emits a fixed delivery-failure metric and uses the separate existing alarm path, without recursively invoking the report job. An email-provider/SNS acceptance is not verified inbox receipt. A shared destination outage can prevent both paths from delivering: preserve the observable failure and operator polling runbook; do not promise an independent notification channel that has not been provisioned and tested.

Synthetic examples (specification only):

| Received | Complete | Partial | Inconclusive | Failed | Other terminal categories | Pending |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | 70 | 5 | 5 | 15 | 2 | 3 |
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

If two pending checks later complete and one fails, revision 2 has complete 72, failed 16, pending 0; received remains 100. Replaying any existing event changes none of these totals. Each other terminal category is retained separately in the actual report, rather than collapsed into a technical failure. Zero-denominator rates are `N/A`. Accounting can be unknown even when the processing outcome is already terminal.

## Approved service-wide alert policy

These owner-approved thresholds are the starting policy for future implementation, separate from the currently deployed resolver alarms. Derive service faults from allowlisted processing reasons; hostile/invalid inputs and unsafe verdicts are not availability failures.

| Condition | Evaluation | Action/recovery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sustained technical failures | At least 20 distinct terminal checks (all outcome categories) observed in each five-minute window; checks with approved technical-failure reasons / those terminal checks >=10% in two consecutive windows | Open one incident; recover after two observed healthy windows. |
| Low-volume faults | Three distinct technical failures in 15 minutes, regardless of total traffic | Open one incident; deduplicate with the rate alarm for the same service/cause. |
| Stuck processing | Accepted check has no terminal outcome after 15 minutes, or reconciliation backlog exceeds its documented processing target | Raise aggregate stuck-work alert; show count and oldest age bucket, never IDs. |
| Missing service telemetry | Two consecutive failed, owned readiness probes at five-minute intervals; or missing expected reporting/heartbeat job | Mark unavailable/unknown, not healthy. Zero customer traffic alone is not an outage. |
| Access/accounting invariant broken | One verified server event for provider use without authority, repeated deduction, or conflicting terminal ledger decision | Critical notification and owner-directed incident runbook; existing runtime fail-closed controls remain authoritative. This policy does not introduce an automatic feature switch, refund or destructive repair. |
| Report missing or incomplete | No completed prior-day report by 09:00 local, or reconciliation incomplete | Alert and label stale/incomplete data; do not emit a fabricated zero-volume report. |

Deduplication key: finite environment/service/cause, excluding customer or request IDs. Send on open, material severity change and verified recovery; if unresolved, at most one reminder per hour and six per 24 hours for that incident. Aggregate suppressed notifications in the daily report. Insufficient data is not proof of recovery. Recovery for a failure-rate incident requires two consecutive observed windows with at least 20 terminal checks each and a rate below 10%; low-volume incident recovery requires 30 minutes with no new distinct technical failure **and** a fresh successful owned readiness probe/processing heartbeat. Stuck-work recovery requires a successful complete reconciliation showing no overdue work. Missing telemetry remains unknown. For incident overlap use one canonical service/cause key and the most severe active condition; transitions between rate and count triggers must not reopen the same incident. Fixed reason categories, not provider exception strings, determine cause. Keep an incident’s state in bounded operational storage, not unbounded metric dimensions. Incident and delivery state is identifier-free aggregate metadata with the approved **at most 90-day** limit, measured from the incident’s first-open time or report’s original cohort date. Retain only finite environment/service/cause/state/revision/attempt-count fields and an internal deterministic delivery key; no customer/check IDs, recipient copies, free text, opaque provider message IDs or provider responses. Retries, reminders and replays never extend expiry. At expiry, disclose that historical correction, uncertain-send reconciliation, suppression totals and recovery linkage are no longer available; a later fault starts a new generation without rebuilding expired state. Longer retention or external identifiers require a new decision.

New service alert windows use validated **server event time**, in half-open UTC five-minute buckets. Evaluate each bucket two minutes after it closes to allow bounded ingestion lag; two consecutive violating buckets target notification within 12 minutes after the start of the first bucket, plus delivery latency. Each check appears only once at its authoritative terminal transition. Events arriving after bucket evaluation update daily correction records and the fixed late-telemetry counter, but cannot retroactively open, reopen or recover an historical incident. Reconciliation/telemetry lag has its own alert; missing/unfinished input cannot qualify a healthy bucket. Low-volume faults use the latest complete 15-minute event-time interval with the same two-minute allowance, deduplicated by check. Stuck processing uses the current reconciliation time versus authoritative acceptance time. Backfill is explicitly marked by the trusted producer and never masquerades as a current incident.

Owned readiness probes use an independent fixed operation code, not a consumer check event. They do not admit a check, dispatch a paid provider or consume any account allowance, and cannot inflate customer service outcomes or cost coverage. Provider-backed end-to-end synthetic checks require their separately authorized test account, accounting and budget scope; classify their observed usage separately using trusted producer metadata. This document neither authorizes such calls nor grants a synthetic allowance bypass.

Initial escalation target remains the same confirmed support mailbox. After 30 minutes unresolved, add the runbook step and severity to the next allowed notification. Automated retries/fallbacks stay bounded. A human may need to resolve credentials/provider outages; there is no promise of staffed 24/7 response. Design targets: detect sustained faults within twelve minutes plus delivery latency and produce the daily report by 08:15 local; these are not measured SLAs.

## Bounded delivery, access and cost controls

The report is a restricted operator view sent only to support@andmorethings.com. Do not attach check-level samples or correlate demographics, states, ZIP codes, languages or individuals. Keep breakdown dimensions finite and reviewed; all metrics must avoid identifiers and dimension cross-products. CloudWatch metric names/dimensions, event size, batch size, reconciliation pagination/checkpoints and execution time require explicit configured bounds in SEC237/243 before activation. An incomplete scan marks coverage incomplete and cannot claim zero backlog or a healthy service.

Owner-approved initial delivery policy: one daily email per environment containing its prior-day report and coalesced corrections; maximum three send attempts for that email revision (initial plus two retries). Use a durable delivery key and checkpoint before retries. Test the provider’s idempotency capability: after an uncertain send without a provider idempotency guarantee, mark delivery `unknown` and reconcile, rather than blindly resending and claiming duplicate-free delivery. Each distinct incident may send open, one severity change, recovery, and at most six hourly reminders in a rolling 24 hours. A global per-environment safety cap of 30 alert-email attempts per rolling hour and 100 per rolling 24 hours includes retries; excess notifications are coalesced into one deferred summary, never an unbounded queue. Critical incidents remain visible in restricted metrics even when notification capacity is exhausted. Each alert notification, including a deferred summary, has a fixed `(environment, incident generation, transition/reminder sequence)` delivery key, at most three send attempts, and the same unknown-send/no-blind-retry rule as reports. Deferred summaries and all retry attempts count against both caps. Prioritize a first critical notice and verified recovery ahead of informational reminders; preserve one coalesced pending record per finite incident scope and disclose suppression. A consumed global cap cannot guarantee immediate delivery: expose the blocked critical notice in restricted monitoring and the operator runbook, rather than claiming it was sent. These approved caps are not yet configured live limits.

Cost warnings observe approved provider budgets: warn at 80% of the configured hard budget, alert at its exhausted/denied state, deduplicate by fixed environment/provider/budget-window scope, and report unknown cost coverage. The operational service cannot establish a new dollar cap or raise customer allowances. Existing provider authority/budget enforcement remains responsible for hard stops. SEC243 must present an incremental AWS/report-delivery cost estimate and reviewed numeric execution/storage limits before resources are activated. A reporting outage must not cause repeated paid analysis, new charges or expanded access.

Initial operator target remains the confirmed support mailbox, owned by the product owner; Lambda owns publisher/accounting faults and infrastructure owns storage/scheduling/delivery faults. The targets are operational design targets, not a staffed response promise. Escalation changes the incident’s information and severity at that same mailbox; no new recipient, contractor or consumer human-review path is implied.
